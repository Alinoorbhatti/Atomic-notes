# The Pygmalion Effect — Index

> The Pygmalion effect is a psychological phenomenon where high expectations of individuals lead to improved performance, while low expectations can lead to diminished outcomes. As a classic example of a self-fulfilling prophecy, it illustrates how beliefs can shape reality through subtle interpersonal communication and behavioral feedback loops.

- **Lenore Jacobson** — Principal of Oak School and co-author of the classroom Pygmalion study.
- **Robert K. Merton** — Sociologist who coined the term "self-fulfilling prophecy" in 1948.
- **J. Sterling Livingston** — Author of the 1969 HBR article "Pygmalion in Management".
- **Albert Bandura** — Psychologist who formulated self-efficacy theory, which is foundational to the Galatea effect.
- **Elisha Babad** — Researcher who extensively studied the Golem effect in classroom settings.
- **Ovid** — Roman poet whose Metamorphoses contains the original myth of Pygmalion.
