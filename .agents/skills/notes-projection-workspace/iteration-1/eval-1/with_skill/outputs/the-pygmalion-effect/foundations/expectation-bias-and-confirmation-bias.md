# Expectation Bias and Confirmation Bias

> Expectation bias and confirmation bias are cognitive filters where preexisting beliefs and predictions cause observers to selectively notice, interpret, and recall information in a way that confirms their expectations.

## Overview

Expectation bias establishes a prior mental template that colors how behavior is interpreted, while confirmation bias leads observers to actively focus on supportive cues while ignoring or discounting counter-evidence. Together, they act as the primary cognitive drivers of the Pygmalion Effect, transforming subjective observer assumptions into objective target outcomes through behavioral confirmation. ## Key Points

- **Cognitive Filtering via Selective Attention**: Observers unconsciously focus on behaviors that align with their expectations while ignoring or discounting counter-evidence, creating a cognitive "tunnel vision."
- **Biased Interpretation of Ambiguity**: Ambiguous or neutral target behaviors are systematically interpreted by the observer to fit their preconceived hypothesis. A minor error from a student expected to underperform is viewed as proof of low ability, while the same error from a student expected to excel is dismissed as an anomaly.
- **Interpersonal Cueing and Differential Treatment**: The observer's internal cognitive biases translate into subtle, non-verbal cues (e.g., tone of voice, posture, and facial expressions) and overt behavior (e.g., offering more feedback and challenges). This differential treatment signals the observer's expectations to the target.
- **Behavioral Confirmation Process**: The target senses these cues, internalizes them into their own self-concept and self-efficacy, and ultimately adjusts their behavior to match the observer's treatment. This creates a self-fulfilling prophecy where the target's performance objectively changes.
- **Reinforcement of the Initial Bias**: When the target's behavior aligns with the observer's initial expectation, the observer views this as external validation of their original hypothesis. They remain blind to their own role in eliciting that behavior, cementing their expectation and confirmation biases.
